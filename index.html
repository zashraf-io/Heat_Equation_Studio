🔥 1. Heat Conduction & Diffusivity

Derived from Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction ( q = − k ∇ u ) and thermal conservation. The parameter β is the thermal diffusivity:

β = k c ρ  •  ∂ u ∂ t = β ∇ 2 u + Q ( x , t ) c ρ

High β diffuses heat rapidly; low β acts like a thermal insulator.

🛡️ 2. Boundary Conditions Compared

Dirichlet: Fixed boundary temperatures u ( 0 , t ) = T 0 , u ( L , t ) = T L . Energy leaves or enters freely through the contacts.

Neumann: Insulated boundaries ∂ u ∂ x = 0 . Heat flux is zero; total thermal energy in the body is strictly conserved!

Robin: Newton's Law of Cooling − k ∂ u ∂ n = h ( u − T ∞ ) . Convective heat exchange with ambient atmosphere.

📈 3. Separation of Variables & Damping

Expanding temperature into spatial eigenfunctions:

u ( x , t ) = ∑ n = 1 ∞ A n e − β ( n π L ) 2 t sin ( n π x L )

High spatial frequencies n damp exponentially at a rate proportional to n 2 . In the Fourier Harmonics preset, high-frequency ripples ( 5 π ) flatten almost instantaneously, leaving only the fundamental harmonic!
